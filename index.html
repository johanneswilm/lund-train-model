Key modelling choices (and why)

  • Decision neutrality. Earlier decisions can be revoked, so every package — including the tunnel — is scored by the same capacity/delay/CBA machinery. Where Trafikverket has ruled (e.g. against the tunnel, 2025–26 [S6][S17]) that is shown as factual context and baked into default cost/risk parameters, which you are free to change. The ANSWER card follows the numbers only.
  • Geography is measured, not guessed. Section lengths, chord detours and the curve radius come from OpenStreetMap rail geometry (Sep 2026) and were cross-checked on a scale-calibrated Google Maps screenshot (200 m scale bar = 76 px → 2.63 m/px): Klostergården–Lund C 1.65 km, Lund C–Stångby 5.14 km (bottleneck ≈ 6.8 km), Malmö–Lund 16.5 km, Kävlinge-origin freight chord ≈ +10 km, Teckomatorp-origin ≈ +27 km, Lund–Stockholm 580 km, min curve radius ≈ 335–355 m just south of Lund C (200 m-window circle fits: 336 m minimum, 353 m over the main arc; the fitted circle tracks the rail rendering on the screenshot). Alt B's new chord links the western Godsstråket (Malmö–Kävlinge, west of Lund) at Kävlinge to the Södra stambanan at Stångby — so the new build crosses north-west of the city; the purely western route (Godsstråket all the way via Åstorp–Hässleholm) already exists but is a much longer detour. At R≈340 m an 80 km/h limit needs ≈220 mm of total cant — within reach of max cant (≈150–160 mm) plus ≈60–70 mm cant deficiency (~0.4–0.5 m/s² residual lateral); much above ~90 km/h is not. The 80 km/h default is therefore geometrically plausible, but verify the linebook value.
  • 4-tracking is a capacity measure, not a speed measure. Every train already slows at Armaturkurvan, and Alt A keeps the curve at grade — so Alt A leaves curve speed untouched by design. Speed requires realignment or Alt C; capacity requires Alt A or B. The model enforces this and shows it in the travel-time module.
  • Capacity = trains/hour from weighted occupation time (run time × factor + dwell × factor + separation buffer + junction conflicts), capped by the design figure, per track pair. Heterogeneity (200 km/h pax vs 90–120 km/h freight) raises occupation time and lowers practical capacity below the 20–22 tph/dir design value — typically to ~12–14 tph/dir mixed.
  • Delays: hourly timetable built greedily (LD first, regional, freight in gaps), then Monte Carlo with exponential primary delays; conflicts resolved first-come-first-served. Punctuality = share of trains ≤5 min.
  • CBA: Swedish ASEK-style defaults (3.5% discount, 40-yr horizon, VoT 550/230/140 SEK/h). Benefits: passenger time savings, reliability (≈1.5 SEK per expected delay-minute per passenger), freight train-hour value, externalities toggles. "Congestion relief" credit uses a rule-of-half generated-traffic approximation when demand exceeds do-nothing capacity.
  • Alt D couples to Alt B: Swedish practice bans freight/diesel from long city tunnels (Citytunneln Malmö precedent), so D cannot remove freight from the equation by itself.

Key facts used (see fact sheet §9 for sources S1–S23)

  • Lund C: ~40 000 pax/day, 6 platform tracks, 3rd busiest in Sweden; junction of Västkustbanan and Södra stambanan [S4].
  • 4 tracks Malmö–Lund since Dec 2023; remaining 2-track bottleneck Klostergården–Lund C–Stångby ~6 km, design 20–22 tph/dir [S1][S2][S7].
  • ~400 passenger + 60–70 freight trains/day; forecast ~700–800/day after Fehmarn Bält (rail 2029) [S4][S8].
  • Freight Malmö↔Stockholm must pass Lund C; Malmö↔Göteborg uses Godsstråket around the city [S3].
  • Oct 2023 decision: 2 new tracks Klostergården–Hässleholm, 250 km/h mixed, 28 bn SEK frame, ~2040 [S7][S13].
  • Cost anchors (2020 pre-study): at-grade 4-track through Lund 5–7 bn; +outer freight track +3–4 bn; tunnel 18–22 bn (rejected) [S5][S6].
  • Tunnel precedents for overrun risk: Hallandsås 1.25→10.8 bn (~11×), Citybanan 16.8 bn/6 km, Citytunneln Malmö 8.5–9.5 bn [S14][S15].

Assumptions that need verification from Trafikverket sources

  • Armaturkurvan speed limit (default 80 km/h) — get the linebook/BIS value. Curvature is measured (R ≈ 335–355 m from OSM + screenshot), so the 80 km/h default is geometrically plausible; the posted limit still needs confirming.
  • Current timetable paths and punctuality at Lund C throat — Trafikverket TIS/Först data or LÖT demand model, for calibration of junction conflicts and delay levels.
  • Alt B1 existing route — confirm line capacity/speeds and single-track meet constraints on Lommabanan, Kävlinge–Teckomatorp and Rååbanan (Eslöv–Teckomatorp via Marieholm, 14.9 km), and the long-term priority-list status of meeting tracks. Model uses the OSM-measured +38.8 km detour (Malmö–Kävlinge–Teckomatorp–Eslöv–Stångby 60.4 km vs 21.6 km via Lund C) — the update draft's "+10–12 km" was the B2 figure.
  • Alt B2 alignment/cost — the new Kävlinge→Stångby chord has no formal Trafikverket study; cost anchors +3–4 bn add-on (2020) vs 10–15 bn standalone (2026) both exposed in the slider. Corridor reservation in north-west Lund municipality + Stångby per the 2024 comprehensive plan.
  • National-plan acceleration — the April 2026 plan front-loads Hässleholm–Lund (1,171 bn SEK frame; four tracks through Lund = key measure [S23]), but Trafikverket's 2026 timetable still pointed to 2043–2048. Confirm the current plan for the corridor; the toggle brackets 2037 vs base 2040.
  • Fehmarn Bält ramp-up (2029 vs German-side ~2034), Öresundståg fleet renewal, Swedish HSR policy status.
  • Demolition/compensation cost at Armaturkurvan; land-value uplift of decking ("Lund+" ~2 bn).
  • Alt C cost (no official estimate; model default 10–15 bn calibrated against new-build alternatives 20–41 bn for ~35 km [S7]).
  • Citytunneln precedents for Alt D: freight/diesel ban, emergency-brake override, storm-Sven 2013 flood near-miss, closure/resilience assumptions [S15][S16]; tunnel settlement/heritage risk to Klosterkyrkan [S17].
  • Occupation factors (dwell/run factors, buffers) are sketch calibration values — ERTMS L2 signalling assumed.

History & status (context — the model scores alternatives independently of it)

2017–2026 timeline and current status
  • 2017: state–municipality agreement fixed four tracks at grade Högevall–Lund C–Stångby, politically foreclosing alternatives.
  • July 2019: a majority of Lund's parties wanted an outer freight track + tunnel [S18].
  • 2020: Lund municipality's Sweco pre-study prices the outer track at +3–4 bn and notes most central negative effects disappear if freight leaves the core [S19].
  • 2023–2026: FörNyaLund campaigns for "yttre godsspår + tunnel", collecting 10,000 signatures for a local referendum (deadline 1 Feb 2026); seminar Jan 2026 [S20].
  • April 2026: national plan 2026–2037 (1,171 bn SEK) front-loads Hässleholm–Lund; four tracks through Lund = the single most important Södra stambanan measure [S23].
  • Status: the outer freight track has never had a formal Trafikverket localization study; Trafikverket maintains the new tracks follow the existing corridor. Benefits accrue mostly to Lund while 4-tracking serves the whole Malmö–Stockholm corridor — freight takes the detour hit.
